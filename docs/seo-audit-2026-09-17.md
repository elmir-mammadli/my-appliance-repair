# MyAppliance indexing audit — September 17, 2026

## Finding

The public site does not currently show a broad technical indexing block. All 73 URLs in the live sitemap returned HTTP 200, allowed indexing, and had a single matching self-canonical URL. This does **not** prove that Googlebot has historically received the same responses, or that Google will index each page.

The main unresolved issue is why Google has deferred crawling most of the site. The subsequently supplied Crawl Stats report shows healthy host availability and a small share of first-time crawls. Together with the successful washer live test, this makes a persistent hosting/access problem less likely and shifts attention toward crawl demand and scheduling. It does not establish a penalty, content rejection, or a problem caused by using Claude.

## Changes made in this audit

- `src/app/sitemap.ts`: removed build-time `lastModified` values. Unchanged pages should not acquire a new content-modification date on every deployment. Omit the field until real update dates are tracked. [Google's lastmod guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/06/sitemaps-lastmod-ping) also notes that Google ignores sitemap `priority` and `changefreq`.
- `src/app/[city]/page.tsx`: linked the five appliance cards with matching service pages to their detail routes, with keyboard focus styling. The air-conditioner card remains unchanged because there is no matching detail route.
- `src/app/services/[slug]/page.tsx`: pointed the service breadcrumb at `/services`.
- `src/components/StructuredData.tsx`: removed the global FAQ markup that was being emitted on articles, privacy, terms, and other pages without those FAQs. Existing page-specific FAQs remain. [Structured-data guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies) requires markup to represent the page's content; this cleanup is not a demonstrated fix for deferred crawling.

These edits are local and have not been deployed.

## Content that needs owner/technician review

The first three items below describe the original live-site findings. The owner subsequently supplied pricing, review counts, and service cities; local corrections and the requested hero exception are recorded in the September 18 follow-up at the end of this report.

1. **Prices disagree:** the visible homepage FAQ says $120–$400, while homepage FAQ schema says $175–$400. Confirm the actual policy before unifying the data.
2. **Review counts disagree:** markup says 38; visible components contain 42 and 60+. Verify current sources and ensure displayed testimonials are accurate. Own-business LocalBusiness review markup is [ineligible for Google's self-serving review stars](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/review-snippet).
3. **Coverage promises disagree:** the coverage section describes Greater New Haven; other copy promises service across all 169 Connecticut towns. Confirm actual travel limits, appointment availability, and any travel charges.
4. **Local experience needs substantiation:** city copy includes neighborhood-specific repair histories and travel claims. Use real jobs, original photos, and verified customer experiences where available. Do not create invented examples to differentiate pages.
5. **Repair advice needs technical fact-checking:** the dryer heating-element article recommends operating with the vent disconnected, and the stove-burner article broadly treats burner caps as interchangeable. Whirlpool's [gas dryer manual](https://www.whirlpool.com/content/dam/global/documents/202408/owners-manual-w11679955-revB.pdf) requires outdoor exhaust; GE's [burner-cap guidance](https://products.geappliances.com/appliance/gea-support-search-content?contentId=17358) should be followed for the specific model. Correct these instructions before promoting the guides.

The articles are substantial in length, and the city pages have distinct prose; simply calling all of them thin or deleting all city pages is not supported by this audit. Google's [AI-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/02/google-search-and-ai-content) focuses on usefulness and reliability rather than which tool wrote the page.

Reported percentages are rounded. The 819 requests include assets and repeat requests, not 819 distinct pages. HTML's 16% share represents approximately 131 HTML fetches, potentially including repeated URLs. Discovery means a requested URL had not previously been crawled; its 4% share is not the percentage of site pages discovered. Do not multiply the separate HTML and Discovery percentages to infer how many new content pages were crawled. Google's [Crawl Stats definitions](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/9679690?hl=en) explain these distinctions.

The report shows recurring crawl activity, predominantly refreshes, with limited first-time crawling. Healthy host status and the successful live test make a persistent availability bottleneck less likely. They cannot exclude every isolated failure or explain Google's exact scheduling choices. Low crawl demand is a plausible working explanation, not a proven content-quality judgment. Google describes page value, popularity, and URL inventory as factors in [crawl demand](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawl-budget).

The JavaScript share does not demonstrate that scripts are consuming a fixed allowance that would otherwise go to new pages. There is no evidence here to justify blocking JavaScript/CSS, changing frameworks, or upgrading hosting. The 2% 404 share needs URL-level context; the already identified broken dishwasher link has a local redirect prepared, but this audit has not identified every URL in that group.

The practical priorities are now to verify the business/content claims, review and deploy the prepared corrections, and improve original evidence on the core service and location pages. After deployment and the existing indexing request, check whether priority URLs receive ordinary Googlebot crawls and how their indexing status changes. Additional mass live tests or repeated indexing requests are not the next step. The exact reason for Google's limited first-time crawling remains unconfirmed.
